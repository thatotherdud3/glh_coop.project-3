PLANT KNOWLEDGE

Vegetable Basics

No jargon, just the basics. Vegetables are the foundation of any decent diet, but you don't need a biology degree to understand why. Here is a simple, informal breakdown of what different plants do for your body and why you should be eating more of them.

THE UNDERGROUND POWERHOUSES

Root Vegetables

Root vegetables like carrots, beets, and radishes anchor you. They absorb nutrients directly from the soil, making them dense with complex carbohydrates, vitamins, and minerals. They are grounding, hearty, and store well for months. If you need sustained energy, this is where you start.

THE SOLAR PANELS

Leafy Greens

Greens like Swiss chard, kale, and spinach are essentially edible solar panels. Packed with chlorophyll, iron, and fibre, they are light on calories but heavy on vitality. Eat them raw to keep their snap, or wilt them down to unlock deeper flavours. You can never really eat too many of these.

THE DEFENCE MECHANISM

Cruciferous

Broccoli, cabbage, cauliflower, and Brussels sprouts belong to the cruciferous family. They bring fibre, vitamin C, and plenty of texture to the table. Roast them for crisp edges, steam them until just tender, or add them raw to salads for extra crunch.

THE FLAVOUR FOUNDATIONS

Alliums

Onions, garlic, leeks, and shallots form the backbone of countless meals. Part of the allium family, they bring aroma and depth to everyday cooking. Cook them gently for mellow sweetness, or use them raw when you want a sharper bite. A little goes a long way.

THE PROTEIN BUILDERS

Peas & Beans

Peas, beans, and lentils are legumes, valued for their plant protein and fibre. Fresh peas bring sweetness and crunch, while dried beans and lentils make soups, stews, and salads more substantial. They're useful pantry staples for affordable, filling meals.

THE COLOUR PALETTE

Fruiting Vegetables

Tomatoes, peppers, aubergines, and courgettes are botanically fruits, but we usually cook with them as vegetables. They offer colour, moisture, and variety. Grill them, roast them, or simmer them into sauces to bring out their different textures and flavours.
